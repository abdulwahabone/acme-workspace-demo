Choosing a filter

Four filters, one refill pack. The difference between them is where the water is treated: in a jug, at the tap, under the sink, or on the pipe coming into the house.

Start with the water you have

Most people buy a filter for one of two reasons: the water tastes of chlorine, or the kettle scales up every few weeks. They lead to different answers.

If it is taste, a jug or a tap filter is enough, and both are cheap to run. If it is limescale, the filter has to sit on the incoming main, because scale forms in the boiler and the pipes long before the water reaches a jug.

Not sure which it is

Boil a kettle of tap water and let it cool. A white film on the surface is scale. A smell like a swimming pool is chlorine.

The four filters

Filter Where it sits Good for Cartridge
Ripple Pitcher Fridge door One or two people, taste only About 2 months
Tap Clip On the spout Rented kitchens, no plumbing allowed About 3 months
Undersink Duo Cupboard under the sink Busy kitchens and small cafes About 12 months
Whole Home Guard Rising main Hard water, scale in the boiler About 24 months

What a filter does not do

None of these make unsafe water safe. They are for taste, sediment and scale in water that is already drinkable. If you have been told to boil your water, keep boiling it.

Running costs

The cartridge is the real price of a filter. A Ripple Pitcher costs £34 once and about £42 a year in cartridges; an Undersink Duo costs more to buy and less per litre to run. 

Ripple Pitcher   £34 + £42/year
